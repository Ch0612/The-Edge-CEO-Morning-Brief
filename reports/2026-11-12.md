## Healthcare, insurance and pharmacy retail

| Company / investment theme | News | Investment considerations |
|---|---|---|
| **Allianz Malaysia (ALLIANZ), Takaful Malaysia (TAKAFUL); MediAsas** | The government reported interest from four major hospital groups in MediAsas, targeting a late-January 2027 national rollout. Allianz Life and Takaful Malaysia were among pilot insurers/takaful operators. Indicative monthly premiums were RM60–550 (p3). | Final products, pricing and hospital participation were not settled. Assess claims experience, distribution and diagnosis-related-group reimbursement rather than assuming higher margins. |
| **Private hospitals: conditional sector exposure** | The hospital groups expressing interest were not named. MediAsas aims to broaden affordable access using standardised benefits and diagnosis-based payment (p3). | **Inferred candidates:** IHH Healthcare and KPJ Healthcare may warrant monitoring, but this edition does not confirm that either is among the four interested groups. Patient volume benefits must be weighed against reimbursement rates. |
| **Big Caring (proposed listing)** | BIMB highlighted institutional demand but also a demanding RM1.08 IPO valuation, reported as 42 times trailing earnings. Longer-term growth may require expansion beyond pharmacy retail (p13). | Broker forecasts and targets are opinions. Evaluate store economics, like-for-like sales and acquisition execution; cornerstone demand does not establish fair value. |

### Further research

- **Broker context in the edition.** BIMB’s Big Caring assessment depends on several years of earnings growth and eventual business expansion (p13). Compare the assumptions with the prospectus.
- Confirm pilot participation and final scheme terms through official notices, and listed companies’ disclosures. [BNM](https://www.bnm.gov.my/) · [Bursa company announcements](https://www.bursamalaysia.com/market_information/announcements/company_announcement)

## Energy, utilities and plantations

| Company / investment theme | News | Investment considerations |
|---|---|---|
| **Dialog (DIALOG)** | Salbiah achieved first gas on 27 October. Dialog reached final investment decision for Fatimah and plans another US$211m, approximately RM860m, of development investment (p6). | Assess production ramp-up, PSC economics, capex commitments, borrowing and cash flow; the funding mix was not finalised. |
| **United Plantations (UTDPLT)** | Q3 profit reached RM290.93m, up 43.1%, on stronger production and firm prices. It declared 30 sen interim plus 15 sen special dividend, payable 8 December (p7). | Monitor yields, realised prices and downstream hedge timing. A special dividend should not be treated as a recurring annual payout. |
| **YTL Power (YTLPOWR)** | CGS argued the sell-off was excessive, distinguishing contracted colocation capacity of 298MW from its larger 850MW valuation assumption and broader AI ambitions (p13). | The assumed capacity is not existing contracted capacity. Test power supply, gas curtailment, tenant demand and regulatory risk rather than adopting a broker target as fact. |
| **Palm-oil supply and export duty** | Analysts forecast inventory drawdown from Q1 2027 after October’s record 3.62m tonnes. December’s CPO reference price was cut to RM4,259.70/tonne, leaving export duty at 10% (p10). | Forecast drawdown depends on weather, output, exports and biodiesel demand. A possible duty waiver was discussed but not implemented in this edition. |
| **PETROS and Samalaju industrial gas** | PETROS agreed gas supply for OCI TerraSus’ Bintulu polysilicon operations (p10). | The named parties are not Bursa-listed equity award recipients. Possible broader infrastructure benefits require disclosed contracts. |

### Further research

- **Broker context in the edition.** CGS and HLIB outline different near-term inventory drivers (p10); CGS’s YTL Power case relies on colocation assumptions (p13). Separate forecasts from contracted operations.
- Track production, exports and duty notices. [MPOB](https://bepi.mpob.gov.my/)
- Check upstream capex and first-gas disclosures. [Bursa company announcements](https://www.bursamalaysia.com/market_information/announcements/company_announcement)

## Consumer, property and new listings

| Company / investment theme | News | Investment considerations |
|---|---|---|
| **Heineken Malaysia (HEIM)** | Q3 profit fell 38.74% to RM69.18m and revenue fell 17.82% to RM539.14m. Lower volumes reflected weak sentiment, higher excise duty and inventory normalisation, expected to conclude in Q4 (p7). | Watch sell-through volumes, affordability, pricing and costs. Destocking completion is management’s expectation, not proof of a demand recovery; no quarterly dividend was declared. |
| **Empire Premium Food (EMPIRE)** | Q2 FY2027 profit was RM13.69m on RM85.23m revenue; profit rose 26.2% quarter on quarter with nearly flat sales. Declared 0.67 sen dividend, payable 8 December; plans 165 outlets by March 2027 (p8). | Compare margin improvements with same-store sales and expansion costs. No year-on-year comparison was provided for this newly listed company. |
| **S P Setia (SPSETIA)** | Q3 profit fell 52.06% to RM32.61m, with lower revenue, no comparable land sale and higher costs. Net gearing improved to 0.30 times; nine-month sales reached RM2.34bn (p8). | Separate recurring development profit from land sales. Track cash conversion, launches and overseas delivery; lower gearing alone does not reverse weaker earnings. |
| **Panda Eyes (proposed ACE listing)** | Signed an underwriting agreement with M&A Securities for a planned January 2027 IPO, with 15 additional stores proposed (p9). | Pricing and final prospectus terms matter. Evaluate store returns, online-sales costs and use of proceeds before inferring listing upside. |
